Microsoft 365 Security Health Check: 15 checks for small teams

By Avinash Sahu · November 2026 · about 30 minutes

Go through these 15 checks with an admin account. Tick each one that passes. Anything you cannot tick, or cannot check, is where to look first.

Download the PDF

Check and why it mattersWhere to lookWhat good looks like
1. MFA is registered for every user
Stolen passwords are the most common way in. MFA stops most of those attacks.		Entra admin center, Protection, Authentication methods, Activity, User registration detailsEvery user registered, or MFA enforced through Security defaults or Conditional Access.
2. Only a few Global Administrators
Every admin account is a high-value target.		Entra admin center, Roles & admins, Global AdministratorA small number of named people (Microsoft recommends fewer than five). None used for daily work.
3. An emergency access (break-glass) account exists
If MFA or a policy locks everyone out, this account gets you back in.		Microsoft Learn: Manage emergency access accountsAt least one cloud-only account, long random password stored offline, sign-ins monitored.
4. Security defaults or Conditional Access is on
Gives a baseline of protections without extra work.		Entra admin center, Overview, Properties, Manage security defaultsOne of the two is enabled. Do not run both at once.
5. Legacy sign-in methods are blocked
Old protocols cannot do MFA, so attackers use them.		Entra sign-in logs, filter by client appNo successful sign-ins from legacy protocols.
6. External auto-forwarding is blocked
Attackers forward mail out to read it later.		Defender portal, Policies, Anti-spam, Outbound spam policy, Automatic forwardingSet to system-controlled or off, unless a business reason is documented.
7. No suspicious inbox rules
Hidden rules can forward or delete mail silently.		Exchange Online PowerShell: Get-InboxRule per mailboxNo rules that forward externally or move mail to odd folders.
8. Shared mailboxes cannot be signed into
A shared mailbox with a password becomes a login anyone can share.		Microsoft 365 admin center, Users, shared mailbox accountSign-in blocked. Access given through delegation instead.
9. Licences match your people
Leavers holding licences cost money. Lapsed subscriptions disable services.		Microsoft 365 admin center, Billing, LicensesNo leavers licensed, no duplicates, no lapsed or disabled subscriptions.
10. Third-party app access is reviewed
Old apps can keep reading mail and files long after they are forgotten.		Entra admin center, Enterprise applications, and user consent settingsNo unknown apps with mailbox or file access. User consent restricted.
11. SPF, DKIM and DMARC are set up
Stops spoofing of your domain and helps your mail reach inboxes.		DNS plus the Defender portal, Email authentication settingsAll three pass. DMARC at least monitoring with reports read.
12. Work devices are encrypted and managed
A lost laptop should not mean lost data.		Intune admin center, encryption reportAll work laptops encrypted, recovery keys stored in Entra ID.
13. The audit log is on
Without it you cannot investigate a suspected breach.		Microsoft Purview, AuditAuditing enabled and you know how far back it goes.
14. External sharing is limited
Anyone-links and unreviewed guests leak files.		SharePoint admin center, Policies, SharingSharing set to the narrowest level the business needs. Guests reviewed.
15. There is a recovery plan
Microsoft keeps deleted items for a limited time. That is not a full backup.		Your own plan, written downYou know how to restore a deleted file, a mailbox and a leaver, and who does it.

How to read your result

  • 13 to 15 ticked: good shape. Re-check every quarter.
  • 9 to 12 ticked: fix the gaps this month, starting with MFA, admins and forwarding.
  • 8 or fewer: treat it as urgent. A review now is cheaper than a clean-up after an incident.

This is a practical starting checklist, not legal or compliance advice. Menu names in Microsoft portals change from time to time.
